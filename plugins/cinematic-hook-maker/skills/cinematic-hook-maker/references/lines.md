## Before and After

Key: `before-after`. A change over time: a skill, a body, a business, a place, a person.

**Beats of the video that follows:** Show the after first, in one frame → Cut back to the before, specific and unflattering → The ugly middle: one real setback → The thing that actually made the difference → Return to the after, now earned.

**Opening pictures:**

- Split frame or match cut: same spot, same pose, then and now
- The finished result held for one beat, then a hard cut to the mess it came from
- A calendar, a counter or a date stamp jumping backwards

**In a scripted film:** Open on the character at their best, then smash cut to the night everything was worse.

**Lines:**

1. This is me today. Twelve months ago I couldn't [simple task].
2. [Result] now. [Starting point] then. The middle is messier than you think.
3. Same room, same person. Only one thing changed.
4. Everyone sees the after. Let me show you the before.
5. I didn't become [result] by working harder. I changed one habit.
6. The difference between these two shots is [time]. And one decision.
7. Old me would have quit right here.
8. If you're where I was a year ago, watch this to the end.
9. Nobody warned me the hardest part comes after the first win.

## The Costly Mistake

Key: `mistake`. A failure, a wrong call or an expensive lesson, told so the viewer avoids it.

**Beats of the video that follows:** The bill: what it cost, stated plainly → The moment it seemed like a good idea → The warning sign that got ignored → The fallout → The rule you follow now.

**Opening pictures:**

- The damage first: the broken thing, the empty account, the ruined take
- A hand hesitating over a button, then pressing it
- Slow push-in on a face realising too late

**In a scripted film:** Open on the consequence (the fire, the empty chair), then rewind to the small choice that caused it.

**Lines:**

10. This one mistake cost me [amount]. It took four seconds to make.
11. I followed the checklist perfectly. The checklist was wrong.
12. Don't do what I did with [thing].
13. It looked like a shortcut. It was a trap.
14. There was a warning. I scrolled right past it.
15. I've had big wins. This flop taught me more than all of them.
16. Thinking about [action]? Watch my [amount/time] disappear first.
17. A two-second shortcut. A two-week cleanup.

## The Turning Point

Key: `turning-point`. One moment that changed direction: a conversation, a discovery, a door closing.

**Beats of the video that follows:** Life on the old track → Pressure building toward quitting → The moment: what was said or seen → The first step on the new track → Where the new track leads.

**Opening pictures:**

- A held frame, then one small detail changes and everything follows
- A character stops mid-stride and turns around
- A phone screen lights up with one message

**In a scripted film:** Freeze on the exact second before the turn, with the line over it, then let the scene play.

**Lines:**

18. I'd already written the goodbye post for [goal].
19. Somebody said six words to me in [place]. I've worked differently since.
20. Everything before this moment, I was doing it wrong.
21. Week one without [old habit] felt worse. Week three felt like a new job.
22. Nobody planned this. One message changed the year.
23. I wasn't looking for an answer. It found me anyway.
24. This is the day I stopped [old way] and started [new way].
25. I almost didn't open the door. I'm glad I did.

## Behind the Curtain

Key: `behind-curtain`. The hidden work behind a finished result: process, failed takes, tools, decisions.

**Beats of the video that follows:** The polished result, briefly → Pull back to reveal the messy setup → The hardest problem and how it was solved → The detail nobody notices → The result again, now seen differently.

**Opening pictures:**

- Camera pulls back from the perfect shot to show the clamps, tape and chaos around it
- Final frame, then rewind effect to version one
- Screen recording of the hundredth attempt

**In a scripted film:** Start on the public face of a person or place, then cut to what happens when the doors close.

**Lines:**

26. This took ten seconds to watch and [time] to make.
27. Pull the camera back ten feet and this is the real set.
28. Version one looked like this. Stay for version forty.
29. My favourite shot of this whole project is on the cutting-room floor.
30. Smooth on screen. Forty-one failed takes off screen.
31. Napkin sketch to final frame, in sixty seconds.
32. Here's my desk at 2 a.m. the night before [thing] launched.
33. The trick is hiding in plain sight. Watch the left side of the frame.

## Against the Grain

Key: `against-grain`. Challenging common advice with a real result or a clear reason.

**Beats of the video that follows:** The rule everyone repeats → What happened when you broke it → Why the rule fails here → When the rule is still right → What to do instead.

**Opening pictures:**

- Everyone in frame walks one way; the subject walks the other
- A checklist with one item crossed out hard
- A 'do not' sign, then the subject doing it, calmly

**In a scripted film:** A character does the one thing the audience has been told never to do, and it works, at first.

**Lines:**

34. Every tutorial says [common advice]. I tested the opposite.
35. I taught [popular belief] for years. I'd like to take it back.
36. The most repeated advice in [field] is the one I ignore.
37. Unpopular opinion: [strong opinion].
38. Great advice for beginners. Terrible advice for my [thing].
39. I deleted [popular practice] from my workflow. Nothing broke.
40. I did it the 'wrong' way on purpose.
41. Everyone said don't. So I measured it.

## The Mirror

Key: `mirror`. A shared everyday struggle, so the viewer thinks 'that's me'.

**Beats of the video that follows:** The familiar moment, exactly as it feels → Why it happens → The small fix → Permission or reassurance → One action to take today.

**Opening pictures:**

- A painfully familiar moment: forty open tabs, a cold coffee, a blank page
- Point of view of the viewer's own situation
- A tiny everyday failure in slow motion

**In a scripted film:** Open on an ordinary, recognisable moment (the alarm, the unanswered text) before anything strange happens.

**Lines:**

42. This one's for everyone who has ever [specific struggle] at 1 a.m.
43. [Relatable situation]. Every single time.
44. The part of [life/work/creating] that never makes anyone's highlight reel.
45. You're not lazy. You're [real reason].
46. Day one is easy. Day one again, after a month off, is the hard one.
47. Ten hours of work and the to-do list got longer. Same.
48. Raise your hand if you also [small habit].
49. Screenshot this for your next bad day.

## Someone Else's Story

Key: `someone-else`. A client, customer, viewer, friend or stranger whose experience carries the point.

**Beats of the video that follows:** Who they are and the problem they brought → What everyone assumed → What was really going on → The change that was made → Their result in their words.

**Opening pictures:**

- Their message or voice note on screen
- Their before photo held up to camera
- Their face reacting to the result

**In a scripted film:** Let a minor character deliver the line that the hero needed to hear.

**Lines:**

50. A viewer sent me this. It changed how I teach [topic].
51. Her brief said [problem]. The real problem was two folders over.
52. The wrong version was already exported. Then someone hit pause.
53. We moved one button. Their [metric] doubled.
54. A customer asked 'why?' and we had no answer. So we rebuilt [product/service].
55. Our worst-performing video paid for the next ten.
56. Between takes, the oldest person on set said one thing. I wrote it on my hand.
57. Everyone asked about the numbers. The interesting part is the reason.

## The Open Loop

**Beats of the video that follows:** The odd detail → The first wrong explanation → Digging deeper, one clue → The reveal → What it means.

**Opening pictures:**

- An object where it shouldn't be
- A frame with one detail circled or slowly pushed into
- A character staring at something we can't yet see

**In a scripted film:** Show the reaction before the thing being reacted to, and hold the reveal for the last shot.

**Lines:**

58. The numbers on [situation] were too good. So I checked.
59. I quietly stopped [common action]. Here's what I noticed first.
60. Look closely at the background. Now watch what happens.
61. It happened three times. On the fourth, I set up a camera.
62. Freeze the frame at two seconds. See it?
63. Seven days of [action]. Day five is where it gets weird.
64. The answer had been in the first frame the whole time.
65. Wait for the last three seconds.

**Beats of the video that follows:** The two paths, side by side → What each would cost → The moment of choosing → What the choice cost anyway → Whether it was right.

**Opening pictures:**

- Two doors, two roads or two screens, and a hand hovering
- A character holding two objects, then putting one down
- A coin in the air

**Lines:**

75. I turned down the biggest offer I've ever had. Here's the math.

## The Ticking Clock

**Beats of the video that follows:** The deadline stated with a number → The goal and why it matters → The first obstacle eats time → The last-second push → Made it, or didn't, and the cost.

**Opening pictures:**

- A countdown on a screen, a phone, a wall clock
- A battery icon dropping into the red
- Sand, water or light running out in frame

**In a scripted film:** Put the time limit on screen in the first shot and show it shrinking at every cut.

**Lines:**
